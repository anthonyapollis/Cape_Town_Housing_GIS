# From satellite imagery to better questions: my Cape Town housing atlas

*An applied portfolio project connecting Python, GIS, BI modelling and clear reporting.*

Housing is a question of location as well as numbers. A lower rent can still mean a difficult household budget when transport, utilities and distance from work are considered. A promising site score also needs an explanation: what drives it, what changes under different priorities, and what still needs checking?

Those questions shaped my Cape Town Housing Opportunity Atlas, now available as a live website.

The project screens 30 candidate precincts against 14 criteria. Readers can switch between efficiency and spatial-redress rankings, compare locations and examine nearby mapped rail and facilities. Satellite imagery adds visual context at city and neighbourhood scales, with an offline map available when imagery cannot load.

I brought the work into a connected publication: an interactive atlas, a 30-page illustrated report, an EPUB and an indexed browser edition. The report also discusses expensive rents, housing delivery challenges, practical interventions and the trade-offs around rent controls.

The technical work spans Python analysis, GIS, scenario and sensitivity analysis, a Power BI semantic model with a star schema and DAX measures, and interactive web presentation. Automated tests and browser checks help keep the outputs consistent as the project evolves. I used AI-assisted development and design during the build.

The most valuable lesson was about evidence. A clear visual can make an uncertain estimate look more definitive than it is. Eight screening criteria remain desktop estimates, and candidate points are not verified parcel boundaries. Satellite imagery helps readers understand context; it does not establish ownership, zoning rights or development feasibility. The modelled capacities are scenarios, not promised homes.

That balance—useful analysis, readable design and explicit assumptions—is the kind of work I want to bring to a remote team.
