# LinkedIn launch post

A useful analysis should help someone ask a better question — and see what to investigate next.

That’s the idea behind my Cape Town Housing Opportunity Atlas, a portfolio project bringing together Python, GIS, scenario analysis and visual storytelling.

The project explores 30 candidate precincts against 14 criteria, with two ranking lenses: efficiency and spatial redress.

I’ve developed the work into a connected set of outputs:

• An interactive atlas with site comparisons and local context.
• A reproducible Python workflow for scoring, scenarios and sensitivity analysis.
• A Power BI semantic model with a star schema and DAX measures.
• A 30-page illustrated report, an EPUB and a browser reading edition.

The report also explores expensive rents, household budgets, practical housing solutions and the trade-offs around rent controls. It separates current rules, published evidence and proposals for further evaluation.

One of the most useful lessons: changing the decision priorities can change the shortlist. Showing that shift clearly is part of the analysis.

This is a desktop screening study. It makes assumptions and evidence gaps visible; it does not establish development approvals or delivery feasibility.
