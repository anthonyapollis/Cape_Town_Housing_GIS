# Alternative LinkedIn post — housing challenges and solutions

Where can Cape Town build more homes — and what would make those homes affordable to the people who need them?

I’ve expanded my Housing Opportunity Atlas report to explore that second question.

The new section covers:

• Expensive rents and the full household budget: rent, utilities and transport.
• Land, infrastructure, finance and operating-cost barriers.
• Social and affordable rental, serviced infill and safe small-scale rental.
• Targeted tenant support and accessible dispute resolution.
• Rent controls: protection for existing tenants, access for newcomers, maintenance and rental supply.

Rent control is a design question with real trade-offs. A freeze, an annual increase limit and a subsidised affordable rent are different policies. The report separates current South African guidance, published evidence and proposals for evaluation.

The publication now has 30 pages, with maps, an affordability illustration, a candidate directory, indexes and linked references.

This is a portfolio screening study. Site capacity is modelled, several inputs require verification, and the proposed policy programme is not an adopted municipal plan.

It demonstrates how I connect Python, GIS and visual reporting to questions people can examine and act on.
